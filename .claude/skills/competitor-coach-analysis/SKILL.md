# Competitor Coach Analysis

Systematically research and compare coaches in your niche — analyze YouTube performance, content strategy, positioning, and audience to surface strategic opportunities.

## When to Use

Invoke this skill when the user wants to:
- Identify who the top coaches are in their niche
- Compare their own channel or brand against competitors
- Find content gaps and underserved topics
- Understand how competitors position their offers
- Build a competitive landscape report before launching a new program or channel

## What This Skill Does

1. **Discovers Competitors** — Searches YouTube and web for active coaches in the same niche
2. **Pulls Performance Metrics** — Retrieves subscriber counts, view totals, upload cadence, and engagement rates
3. **Analyzes Content Strategy** — Reviews top-performing videos, recurring topics, and title/hook patterns
4. **Maps Positioning** — Compares messaging, target audience, offer structure, and price signals
5. **Delivers Actionable Report** — Produces a structured analysis with opportunities ranked by impact

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Clarify the Niche

Ask the user (if not already provided):
- "What type of coaching do you offer?" (e.g., fitness, business, career, mindset, nutrition, relationships)
- "Who is your target audience?" (e.g., corporate women 35–50, early-stage founders, new moms)
- "What are 2–3 keywords that best describe your coaching niche?"
- "Are there any specific competitors you already know about?"

### Step 2 — Discover Competitors

Search YouTube for coaches in the niche using combinations of:
- `[niche] coach`
- `[niche] coaching`
- `online [niche] coach`
- `[niche] mentor`
- `[target audience] [niche]`

Target a mix of channel sizes:
- **Emerging** (1K–50K subscribers) — closest current peers
- **Mid-tier** (50K–500K) — aspirational benchmarks
- **Top-tier** (500K+) — market leaders setting category norms

Aim for 5–8 competitors total.

### Step 3 — Collect Channel Data

For each competitor retrieve:
- Subscriber count and total view count
- Number of videos and average upload frequency
- Date channel was created (growth trajectory)
- Top 5–10 most-viewed videos (titles, view counts, publish dates)
- 5 most recent videos (to gauge current focus and cadence)
- Average views per video (last 10 uploads)
- Engagement signals: like-to-view ratio, comment volume on top videos

### Step 4 — Analyze Content Strategy

For each competitor identify:
- **Core content pillars** — the 3–5 themes they return to repeatedly
- **Best-performing formats** — talking head, tutorials, vlogs, interviews, Q&A
- **Title/hook patterns** — emotional triggers, numbers, specific promises
- **Content gaps** — topics their audience asks about in comments that they don't cover
- **Posting consistency** — regular schedule vs. sporadic

### Step 5 — Research Positioning and Offers

Search for each competitor's website and social profiles to find:
- Their stated niche and target audience
- Primary offer (1:1 coaching, group program, course, membership, community)
- Price signals (publicly listed or estimated from context)
- Unique selling proposition and core promise
- Brand tone (professional, conversational, motivational, educational)
- Social proof (testimonials, case studies, credentials highlighted)

### Step 6 — Build the Report

Structure the output as follows:

---

#### Market Overview
- Niche size and growth signals (search volume trends, channel growth rates)
- Dominant content formats in this space
- Pricing range across the competitive set

#### Competitor Profiles
One card per competitor with:
- Channel/brand name and URL
- Audience size and growth rate
- Top content themes
- Core offer and positioning
- Standout strength and notable weakness

#### Comparison Matrix
A table comparing all competitors on:
| Competitor | Subscribers | Avg Views | Upload Freq | Top Topic | Offer Type | Price Signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

#### Content Gap Analysis
- Topics frequently requested in comments but rarely addressed
- Keywords with search demand but low competition among these channels
- Audience pain points not yet well served

#### Strategic Opportunities
Rank 3–5 specific, actionable recommendations:
1. **[Opportunity title]** — What to do, why it works, which competitor gap it fills

---

## Output Notes

- Always cite sources (channel URLs, video links) so the user can verify
- Flag if data was unavailable or estimated
- Keep the tone analytical and objective — avoid making the user feel behind; frame gaps as opportunities
- If the user's own channel or brand is provided, include a "Your Position" card alongside competitors

## Example

> User: "I'm a mindset coach for burned-out corporate professionals. Can you analyze my competitors?"

→ Search YouTube for "mindset coach burnout", "corporate burnout coach", "workplace stress coach". Pull the top 6 channels. Retrieve their metrics, top videos, and website offers. Identify that most competitors focus on general stress tips but few offer structured 90-day programs for senior leaders specifically. Produce a report recommending the user position around that underserved segment with long-form case study content.
